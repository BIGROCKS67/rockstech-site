What I build

  1. Intelligent automation

    The copy-pasting, the chasing, the weekly report nobody reads until it’s late. I wire those jobs up so they run on their own, and your people get their afternoons back.

  2. AI agents

    Assistants for your customers and task-runners for your team. Agents that look things up, fill in the forms, chase the replies and hand over to a person when it matters.

    Your CRM, your inbox, your spreadsheets and that one system from 2009. I connect them so data gets typed in once and lands everywhere it should.

  4. Digital product development

    From a sketch on a napkin to something people log into. Prototypes in days, real products in weeks, with the unglamorous parts (logins, payments, hosting) done properly.

    You know something has to change but not where to start. We sit down, map where the hours and the money go, and you leave with a plan you could hand to any developer. Ideally me.

Tender pricing engine

Lesson Let the AI read and the code decide. The AI never sets a price.

  1. Tender pricing engine: AI reads tender packs, code checks and prices them into a workbook and PDF. Lesson: Let the AI read and the code decide. The AI never sets a price.
  2. AML risk platform: Company, sanctions and media checks, with an AI-written report. Lesson: The report goes through rule checks before anyone sees it.
  3. Luxury rental system: Bookings, payments, logins and admin, live in the cloud. Lesson: Live payments meant security work before features.
  4. Real-estate CRM suite: CRM with HR, accounts, conveyancing and mortgage portals. Lesson: Several portals are front-end demos with partial backends.
  5. AI lead engines: Find and score leads, then draft the outreach. Lesson: Drafting is easy. The legal checks are the real work.
  6. Private lead hub: Scores UK companies from about 80 public data feeds and drafts outreach it can never send. Lesson: I built a hard no-send rule so nothing goes out by accident.
  7. Personal health agent: AI agent with tools, a Telegram bot and voice notes. Lesson: It fails over between providers, because one always goes down at the wrong time.
  8. Tool-using trading assistant: AI agent with 37 tools and credit billing. Lesson: Billing per call had to be built before anyone could use it.
  9. Autonomous research loop: AI agents propose ideas and a fixed judge scores them. Lesson: I took the model out of the verdict. It graded its own work too kindly.
  10. Small-business websites: About 45 sites from brief to live domain, with local SEO. Lesson: Moving a client off an old host is where most of the time goes.
  11. Multi-market forecasting: Forecasts on currency pairs, gold, live cattle and Gulf shares. Lesson: Emerging market currency pairs were the hardest to get anything from.
  12. Football prediction app: Models for goals, shots, fouls and cards, on web, iOS and Android. Lesson: A weekly retrain is only useful while it runs. A scheduled one quietly broke.
  13. Company accounts parser: Reads daily filed accounts and joins them to the full UK company file. Lesson: Public data is messy. Dedupe and quality reports matter more than the parser.
  14. AI image studio: Trains and runs custom image models, with an API and video steps. Lesson: Built and tested, then shelved. Not every build should be a product.
  15. Receipt splitter: Reads a receipt photo and splits it into items. Lesson: OCR gets you most of the way. People fix the rest.
  16. Offline learning app: A learning app that works without a connection. Lesson: Offline-first changes every data decision.
  17. Self-hosted GPU setup: A multi-machine training rig with a private network, watchdogs and a thermal guard. Lesson: Watchdogs should read a job's output, not whether it's running.
  18. Strategy search engine: Writes trading rules, tests them on data it has never seen, then paper-trades the survivors. Lesson: One day it had 202 survivors that were really three ideas. Near-copies now count as one.
  19. Backtest engine: Replays strategies through a paper exchange with funding and slippage charged. Lesson: A target touched isn't a target filled. Fills only count when price trades through.
  20. Reality-gated research: Tests trading ideas with every trial logged and costs always in. Lesson: Most ideas died once costs were counted. The log keeps the dead ones.
  21. Venue check: Re-runs every strategy on the exchange it will really trade on. Lesson: An edge priced on one exchange didn't always survive on another.
  22. Trading terminal: Live market data from several exchanges on one dockable screen. Lesson: Real-time data is mostly about handling feeds that drop.
  23. Multi-book order handler: Keeps requested, approved and filled orders apart and refuses stale instructions.
  24. On-chain trading bot: Builds, simulates and sends its own swaps, with a separate guard that can kill it. Lesson: The guard runs as its own process so the bot can't overrule it.
  25. Copy-trading app: iOS app that copies tracked traders and sends push alerts. Lesson: Taken to an app-store build, then the company shut down.
  26. Wallet-tracking collectors: Track and score large-wallet behaviour on a perps exchange. Lesson: A process can be up and doing nothing. Watchdogs now read the output.
  27. Crypto price forecaster: Bitcoin forecasts over several horizons, drawn as a fan of outcomes. Lesson: The first version was barely better than a coin flip. I rebuilt the whole architecture.
  28. Short-horizon signal models: 1, 2 and 4 hour direction calls that go quiet when unsure. Lesson: New versions run in shadow beside the live one before they're trusted.
  29. Chart-pattern AI: Vision models that read price charts, served by API. Lesson: Built end to end, then parked for simpler models.
